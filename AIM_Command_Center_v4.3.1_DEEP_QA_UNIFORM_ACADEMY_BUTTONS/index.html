How to Use AIM™ Rx

There are no additional assessment questions in Rx. Complete Dx, then Rx automatically generates the evaluation and prescription.

How is the AAI calculated?

AAI = 100 × Σ(scores) / 55

In plain English, AIM™ Dx scores the agent across 11 authority dimensions. Each dimension receives a score from 1 to 5. We add those 11 scores together. The highest possible total is 55 because 11 × 5 = 55.

We then divide the agent's total by 55 and multiply by 100. That simply converts the result into an easy-to-read 0–100 comparison scale.

Simple example
If the 11 scores add up to 33: 100 × 33 ÷ 55 = 60. The agent's AAI is therefore 60, which falls in the Elevated Autonomy Exposure band.

Important: AAI is not a safety grade and it does not mean “60% dangerous.” It is a structured measure of how much autonomy and authority are concentrated in the agent. AIM™ Rx then explains what is driving that exposure and what should be changed.

  1. Read the Situation Analysis to understand the agent's overall authority profile.
  2. Review the Exposure Drivers to see which authority dimensions are creating the greatest concern.
  3. Follow the Recommended Remedy in priority order.
  4. Give the Agent Modification Specification to the responsible business, engineering, security, or governance teams.
  5. Apply the A/G/H/X boundaries so the agent knows what it may do autonomously, what is gated, what remains human-reserved, and what is prohibited.
  6. Reassess in Dx after the architecture actually changes. The new score should reflect the modified agent—not a paper adjustment to the old score.

How to Use the Authority Diagnostic

1Describe the agentName the agent, accountable human owner, and authorized purpose.
2Complete Objective CalibrationAnswer observable capability questions. Do not estimate a risk score.
3AIM™ derives the 11 evidence scoresThe evidence becomes the minimum defensible authority score for each dimension.
4Review the 11 dimensionsYou may raise a score when additional authority or consequence exists. Evidence floors prevent downward scoring.
5Calculate Final AAIAIM™ records Evidence-Based AAI, Final AAI, and any upward variance.
6Continue to AIM™ RxApply governance controls to reduce residual exposure without rewriting the underlying authority.
Core principle: Controls may reduce residual exposure. They do not erase the authority the agent actually possesses.
